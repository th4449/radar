# Texas newspaper public notices

- Source id: `tx_public_notices`
- Site: https://www.texaspublicnotices.com
- Operator: Texas Press Association, per the site's Terms of Use
- Checked: November 19, 2026
- Result: **restricted. No collector will be written. This needs the owner's decision.**

This was the plan's first Texas feed and its best statutory signal, the 30 day tax abatement notice.

## What the terms say

Terms of Use: https://www.texaspublicnotices.com/Terms-of-Use.aspx

- Permission covers viewing the site and downloading or printing single pages for personal, non-commercial use. The terms then say "No other use is permitted."
- They forbid putting the content in "any database, compilation, archive or cache."
- They forbid screen scraping, spidering and "any other automated means to collect information from the site."
- They forbid searching the site with any tool other than its own search or an ordinary web browser.
- They set liquidated damages for each of those: $100 per notice or $10,000 per incident, whichever is greater.
- The operator may block addresses that show bot-like activity.

Both the automated-access wording and the damages clause were read twice, once by a research pass and once directly.

## Robots rules

`https://www.texaspublicnotices.com/robots.txt` allows `/` for ordinary agents and closes the whole site to a list of named crawlers. The robots file is more permissive than the terms. The terms govern.

## What the site offers

- Keyword search with county, city, publication and date filters. Notices from the past 12 months are in the main search and older ones in an archive search.
- "Smart Search", an email alert for saved searches. The terms say the operator may charge a fee for it. Its sign-up page, price and terms were not read.
- No RSS feed or API was found.

## What this rules out

- Any collector for this site.
- Storing notice text from this site in our archive or database, even when a person copies it by hand. The terms forbid databases and archives in general, not only automated ones.

## Options for the owner

1. Ask the Texas Press Association for written permission. The terms list a contact address on the site. **The owner sent the request on November 23, 2026. No answer yet.**
2. Have a reviewer subscribe to Smart Search alerts and read them. Whether a signal may then be recorded with a link back, and no copied text, is a question about these terms that the owner should settle, with counsel if needed.
3. Go to the primary record instead. An abatement needs a public hearing and a posted agenda, so commissioners court and city agendas (milestone M8) carry the same event. The lead time there is the agenda posting period, which is shorter than 30 days.
4. Check individual newspapers' own notice pages. Each has its own terms.
